# nsaids-and-energy-drink-dashboard
Dashboard analyzing NSAID and energy drink use among 45 health students at Gombe State University it's built with Excel and Python for Haven Diagnostics Research Lab.
NSAID & Energy Drink Use Among Health Students — Gombe State University

Client: Haven Diagnostics Research Lab
Date: July 2026
Tools: Excel (data cleaning & dashboard), Python (analysis)

Overview

A survey-based dashboard built for a research lab investigating the practice of combining NSAIDs (painkillers) with energy drinks among health students at Gombe State University, Northern Nigeria. The study surveyed 45 health students (average age 22) to understand usage patterns, awareness of risks, and perceived academic impact.

My Role

Cleaned and structured the raw survey data in Excel, ran the analysis in Python, and designed the final dashboard in Excel to present findings to the research lab in a clear, decision-ready format.

Key Findings
- The majority of respondents reported never having combined NSAIDs with energy drinks, though a notable minority had.
- NSAID use skewed toward occasional rather than daily consumption, though daily use was still common enough to flag.
- Self-medication and peer/family recommendations were leading sources of NSAID use — clinical guidance (doctors, pharmacists) played a smaller role than expected.
- Despite risk exposure, most students felt adequately informed about the risks of NSAID and energy drink combinations — a gap worth further investigation given actual usage patterns.
- Coca-Cola was the most commonly consumed energy/caffeinated drink among a fairly fragmented set of brands.
- Opinion was split on whether energy drinks improve academic performance, skewing toward agreement.
